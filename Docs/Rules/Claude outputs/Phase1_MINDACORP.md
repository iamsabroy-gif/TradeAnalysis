## Why the verdict

- **Check 1 is open, not adverse.** Every sub-condition that *would* trigger a fail — auditor walking out mid-term, a qualified/adverse opinion, an active fraud probe — comes back negative on every source checked. The only gap is a number (legal fees ÷ audit fees) that simply isn't published anywhere I could reach, including the full Annual Report PDF (it's too large to fetch in one pass through the tools available here). It stays a hold, not a pass, until that figure is sourced from page-level Annual Report notes.
- **Check 3 is open for the same reason** — the RPT *disclosure quality* looks fine (arm's-length language, subsidiary loan terms stated, AOC-2 exemption correctly claimed), but no total ₹ figure was found to test against the 5%-of-revenue line.
- **Check 4 passes with a caveat worth flagging honestly:** the contingent-liability figures came from a secondary extract of the Annual Report notes rather than the full note in one piece, and that extract flagged itself as possibly incomplete. The margin to the 15% limit is large enough (3.1% vs. 15%) that even a meaningfully bigger undisclosed figure would be unlikely to flip the verdict, but this should be re-verified against the primary note directly before this check is treated as fully closed.
- **Checks 2, 5 and 6 are clean on their own terms** — zero promoter pledge, strong and improving cash conversion (cash generated has run *ahead* of reported profit every year for five years), and a single, personal-reasons CFO transition with a named, in-place replacement.

## What the data does NOT let us conclude

- **Check 1:** Cannot confirm the legal-and-professional-charges-to-audit-fees ratio (fails only if >5x, or a >2x YoY surge in legal fees) — these two line items live in the Annual Report's Notes to Accounts ("Other Expenses" and "Auditor's Remuneration") and were not retrievable through the sources available in this session. Pulling the FY24-25 or FY25-26 Annual Report PDF directly (via SparkMinda / BSE) and reading those two notes would close this.
- **Check 3:** Cannot confirm RPT as a % of revenue — the total sales + purchases figure with related parties (AR "Related Party Transactions" note) was not retrievable. Same document, different note, would close this.
- Note on Check 1's regulatory-action sub-item: the "no SEBI/CBI/ED action found" finding is a **web-scan result, not a filing-level confirmation** — absence of evidence in a scan isn't proof of absence, so treat it as a reasonably clean signal rather than a certified clearance.

## Plain-English summary

**The short answer:** We can't give Minda Corporation a clean pass or a rejection yet. Four of the six honesty checks came back clean — no promoter borrowing against their shares, healthy real cash generation, a stable finance team, and nothing alarming turned up on the auditor or regulatory front. But two checks are stuck open because two specific numbers just weren't published anywhere we could find them, and the rule here is strict: if we can't verify a number, we don't guess — we hold.

Here's what each open item means in plain terms. One check compares how much a company spends on lawyers versus how much it pays its independent auditor — a company suddenly spending far more on legal fees than audit fees can be a sign of hidden litigation or a regulatory fight brewing behind the scenes. We simply don't have Minda Corporation's numbers for either figure, so we can't run that comparison. The other open check looks at "related-party transactions" — money moving between the company and businesses connected to its own promoters or management. Done properly and at fair prices, this is completely normal; done badly, it's a classic way for insiders to quietly move value out of a public company. Minda Corporation says its dealings are all at arm's length, but we don't have the actual rupee total to check that claim against its revenue.

On the good side: the promoters haven't pledged a single share as loan collateral (a real plus — pledging is a common early warning of promoter financial stress), and the numbers show the company has been turning **more** than 100% of its reported profit into actual cash in the bank over the last five years — a strong sign the earnings are real, not just accounting entries. The one finance-chief change we found looks routine (a departure for personal reasons with a named successor already in place), not a red flag.

**So what does this mean for you?** This isn't a rejection — nothing found so far points to a problem. It's an "incomplete file," and the honest move is to say so rather than paper over the gap with an assumption. The next step is pulling the two specific notes (legal/audit fees, and the related-party transactions total) from Minda Corporation's latest Annual Report directly, after which this can move to a clean CLEARED TO PHASE 2 or, if either number turns out to breach a threshold, a REJECT. Either way, a CLEARED verdict here would only ever mean "honest and stable enough to study further" — never a buy signal.
