# Stock Analysis Question Set

A structured set of test questions to understand a company before investing. Each section maps to the Qualitative Research Checklist (Business & Management, Economic Moat, PESTLE) and to the principle that a good "story" must translate into financial success.

---

## Section A — Red Flags & Governance

_Checklist: Business & Management (management character)_

1. Have there been any regulatory actions (SEBI, RBI, SFIO), auditor qualifications, or auditor resignations in the last 3 years?
2. What is the promoter pledge %, and has it changed materially?
3. Any related-party transactions that are large or unusual relative to revenue?
4. What is the trend in contingent liabilities, and is any material litigation disclosed in the Annual Report?
5. Is there a gap between reported profit and cash flow from operations — is profit converting to cash?
6. Have there been frequent changes in CFO/auditors, or any restatements of past accounts?

---

## Section B — 3-Year Evolution

_Checklist: Business & Management (what the company does & expansion)_

7. How have the core business segments shifted in revenue mix over 3 years?
8. What capex was committed vs. actually deployed, and toward what?
9. What major project wins, order-book additions, acquisitions, or divestments occurred?
10. What negatives or concerns surfaced (margin compression, lost clients, delays)?

---

## Section C — Expansion Plans (1-Year / 3-Year)

_Checklist: Business & Management (evaluate expansion plans)_

11. What specific projects/capacities are guided to come online in 1 and 3 years?
12. How is the expansion funded — internal accruals, debt, or equity dilution?
13. What quantified milestones has management set (capacity, revenue, margin targets)?
14. What is the expected asset turn / payback on the new capex?

---

## Section D — Management Commentary

_Checklist: Business & Management (management character)_

15. What did the most recent concall/MD&A emphasize as priorities and risks?
16. Has the tone shifted vs. prior quarters (more cautious / more confident)?
17. What is the capital allocation stance — dividends, buybacks, reinvestment, debt reduction?

---

## Section E — Quantitative Product Metrics

_Checklist: Verifying the "story" translates to financials_

18. For each key product/segment: 3-year trend in revenue, volume, realization, and segment margin.
19. What is the gross / EBITDA / net margin trajectory, and what drove each move?
20. What is the balance-sheet health: debt/equity, interest coverage, working-capital cycle?
21. How does valuation compare to sector peers (P/E, P/B, EV/EBITDA) — cheap or expensive, and why?

---

## Section F — Debt, Leverage & Inter-Corporate Exposure

_Checklist: Business & Management (management character / capital discipline) + Economic Moat (financial resilience). Extends the headline debt/equity read in Q20 into a full balance-sheet-risk deep-dive, and adds the inter-corporate lending/guarantee angle that ratio metrics hide. For a net-cash company, run Q22–Q26 as "is there any debt, and why?" and put the weight on Q27–Q28 (where idle cash and inter-corporate exposure are the real questions)._

22. **Quantum & trend.** What are gross debt and net debt (net of cash) over 3 years, and the trend in net-debt/EBITDA and debt/equity? Is leverage rising, flat, or being paid down — and does that match the Q17 capital-allocation stance?
23. **Maturity & refinancing risk.** What is the maturity profile — short-term vs. long-term borrowings, and how much principal falls due within the next 12 months? Can near-term repayments be met from cash + operating cash flow, or is refinancing required?
24. **Cost of debt & credit rating.** What is the average cost of borrowing, the current credit rating and outlook, and have there been any downgrades, rating watches, or negative-outlook actions in the last 3 years?
25. **Security, covenants & structure.** How much debt is secured vs. unsecured, what assets are pledged as collateral, and are there financial covenants (e.g. leverage or interest-cover thresholds) — with any breach, waiver, or reset disclosed?
26. **Interest-service adequacy.** Is interest coverage (EBIT/interest) comfortable through a downturn given the cyclicality of the order book, and how has coverage trended vs. Q20?
27. **Loans & advances GIVEN to other companies.** Has the company extended inter-corporate deposits, loans, or advances to subsidiaries, JVs, associates, or related parties? How large relative to net worth, are they interest-bearing and performing, and is recoverability or write-off risk flagged? (Cross-reference the related-party disclosures in Q3.)
28. **Guarantees & debt links to OTHER companies.** Has the company given guarantees, letters of comfort, or security on behalf of other entities (subsidiaries, JVs, group companies), or borrowed from group/related parties or unusual sources? Quantify the off-balance-sheet contingent exposure and reconcile it against the contingent-liability trend in Q4.

## Section G — Moat & External Forces

_Checklist: Economic Moat + PESTLE_

29. What is the moat (brand, scale, licenses, switching costs), and is it widening or eroding?
30. PESTLE scan: which political / economic / social / technological / legal / environmental factors most affect the thesis?
31. Who are the key competitors, and how is market share moving?

---

## Section H — Vendors, Outsourcing & Tendering

_Checklist: Economic Moat (make-vs-buy, vertical integration) + Business & Management (execution) + PESTLE (policy). Each query is framed to feed the moat, margin, working-capital, execution-risk, governance, and policy lenses used elsewhere in this set — not to catalogue suppliers._

32. **In-house vs. bought-out (moat / value-add).** What share of product or order value is built in-house vs. subcontracted or bought out — i.e. the value-addition or indigenous-content %? Is the trend toward more in-house (deepening moat) or more outsourcing (thinner, assembler-like model)?
33. **Critical dependency & single-sourcing (execution risk).** Which critical components, subsystems, or services are outsourced, and are any single-sourced or import/foreign-OEM–dependent (Transfer-of-Technology reliance)? What is the concession/lead-time exposure if a key vendor slips?
34. **Award mechanism & concentration (governance / cost).** How is vendor work awarded — open tender, nomination, or long-term agreements — and how concentrated is spend among the top few vendors? Higher single-source or nomination share raises both cost and continuity risk.
35. **Related-party vendors (governance).** Are any material vendors related parties, promoter-group entities, JVs, or associates? Cross-reference against the related-party disclosures flagged in Section A.
36. **Margin linkage (financials).** How does outsourcing show up in the P&L — bought-out material/subcontracting as % of sales — and how has that moved gross/EBITDA margin? Does rising outsourcing explain any margin compression or expansion in Section E?
37. **Working-capital linkage (financials).** What is the vendor-side working-capital impact — advances paid to vendors, trade-payable days, and inventory/WIP build for long-cycle orders? Does the payables cycle fund or strain the working-capital picture in Section E?
38. **Delivery & quality risk (execution).** What vendor-linked execution problems have appeared — delivery delays, quality rejections, liquidated damages, or a delayed sub-supplier stalling a flagship order? Tie to the delays/concerns noted in Section B.
39. **Forward outsourcing & policy strategy (PESTLE / expansion).** What is the stated go-forward vendor strategy — indigenization/import-substitution targets, offset obligations, MSME/ancillary vendor-base development — and how do policy shifts (e.g. defence positive lists, local-content mandates) change the make-vs-buy calculus and the addressable value the company can retain in-house?

## Section I — Return-Requirement Analysis

_Checklist: Business & Management — reframed from a return-guarantee question into a testable assumptions question_

40. Break down what a ~20% annualized return (CAGR) from today's price would require:
    
    - (a) what earnings growth rate,
    - (b) any change in valuation (P/E re-rating or de-rating),
    - (c) dividends.
    
    Then assess how those assumptions compare to the company's actual 3–5 year history and its sector — flag which assumptions look aggressive and what would have to go right.
    
    > **Note:** This question deliberately avoids asking "will I get 20%?" — no analysis can promise a specific return. Instead it asks "what has to be true for 20% to be plausible, and is that a reasonable bet or a hopeful one?"
    

---

## Section J — Forward Guidance vs. Track Record

_Checklist: Business & Management_

41. What is management currently guiding on growth, margins, and market dynamics?
42. Compile past guidance vs. actual delivery over 3+ years — do they consistently meet, beat, or miss?
